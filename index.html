How to read this — and what it cannot show

This is a descriptive summary of one office's closed case file. It counts appearances; it does not measure rates. Because we hold only cases that ended in exoneration, there is no denominator: we cannot see how many cases each prosecutor tried in total, so a prosecutor appearing five times here may have tried five hundred cases or fifty.

Recurrence is therefore a place to look, not a finding. It may reflect caseload volume, seniority, assignment to homicide or other units where exonerations concentrate, or the era and jurisdiction in which someone worked. Nothing here establishes individual misconduct.

Bands with fewer than three cases are shown but should not be compared. Dates are drawn from the case file as recorded and a small share of records are incomplete; cases missing arrest or release dates are excluded from years-lost totals and noted in the count.
